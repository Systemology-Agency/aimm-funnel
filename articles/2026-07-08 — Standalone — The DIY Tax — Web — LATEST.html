The DIY Tax

Why building your own AI system alone costs more than it saves. I paid this tax first, in hours, so you don't have to guess what it costs.

LC
Leo Chen
AI Money Machine · 3 min read

The agents who worry me most aren't the ones ignoring AI. They're the ones staying up until midnight trying to build it themselves.

Not the ones who never opened ChatGPT. The capable ones. The ones who've hit 20-plus deals a year with no assistant, because they've never needed help figuring anything out before.

But here's the thing: the instinct that makes you good at this job, the "I'll figure it out myself" instinct, is the exact instinct that turns AI into a second unpaid job.

You watch a tutorial Saturday morning. You wire ChatGPT to Zapier to your CRM. Something breaks Sunday night. You never open it again.

I call it the DIY Tax. Every hour you spend building it alone is an hour that didn't go into a showing, a call, or the follow-up that actually closes the deal. And you're paying it in the one currency you have the least of.

I know because I paid it first

It took me 245 hours, over three months, nights and weekends, to build the system that now runs both my businesses. I logged the hours as I went. No rounding it down after the fact.

What the gap actually looks like

Let me give you an example. A buyer walks three homes with you Saturday and doesn't write an offer. Built right, the thank-you goes out that night in your own voice, the three-day nudge fires on its own, the week-later check-in lands while you're at a different showing. Built alone, which is most DIY builds, you're the one remembering to send it at 9 PM on a Tuesday. Half the time, you don't.

That gap, between you remembering it and it just happening, is the DIY Tax in one sentence.

Treat it like a new hire.

Open Claude Cowork and start a new project, just for your follow-up system. Then feed it everything: your farm, your niche, three words for how you talk, and the connectors it needs to run, your CRM, your email, your calendar.

You are not asking a chatbot for one follow-up text. You are training an employee to run this for you, every time a buyer walks away, for the rest of your career.

That's the first hour of the 245 you don't have to spend, because I already spent them.
