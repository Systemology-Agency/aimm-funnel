Day-One Training Checklist
Feed Your AI Employee

Train It Once. Then It Works Like You.

Maybe you are thinking: I am just a real estate agent, I do not know how to get it there. Here is how. You would not hand a new assistant the keys with zero context. Give your AI the same things, in this order, and it stops guessing.

This is the part nobody shows you. An AI is not smart about your business until you feed it your business. Work down this list. Check things off as you go. You do not need all of it on day one, but the more you give it, the more it sounds and acts like you instead of a stranger.

Your AI is trained0 / 0
1Who You Areso it speaks as you
Your market. The cities and neighborhoods you farm, your price bands, your average sale price.
Your voice. 5 to 10 emails you have actually sent. Tell it: study how I open, close, and sound. Do not copy, just match me.
Your positioning. Who you serve, what you are known for, what you never say. One paragraph is enough to start.
Your goals. GCI target, deal target, the number you are chasing this quarter. Now it can build your Command Center around real numbers.
2Your Dataso it stops asking you
Your contacts. Your sphere and past clients, or an export of your CRM. This is who it follows up with.
Your active pipeline. Current leads, appointments, and deals in escrow, with where each one stands.
Your listings and transactions. Live listings, recent solds, the details it needs to write about them.
Your documents. Point it at your Drive: presentations, market reports, past emails. Mine grew to 5 gigs and thousands of files. Start with what matters most.
3Your Toolsso it can reach out
Email. Connect your inbox through the official integration so it can read, sort, and draft.
Calendar. So it knows your availability and can tee up appointments.
CRM and MLS / IDX. So new leads flow in and it can pull real properties for a deal of the week.
One safety rule, no exceptions

Never paste raw passwords into a chat window. Connect tools through their official sign-in or a password manager, the same way you would give a real assistant limited access, not your master password. Start every tool with read or draft access, not send-and-delete, until you trust it.

4Your Rulesso it never goes rogue
What it can do on its own, and what it must show you first. Start with draft-only. Nothing sends without you.
Your schedules. When email triage runs, when the deal of the week goes out, when it sends you a summary.
Your SOPs. The step-by-step for the things you do the same way every time. Write it once, it repeats it forever.
Memory. Tell it to remember your corrections. Fix a thing once, it should not need fixing twice.

That is the whole job. Not a course, not code. You are onboarding an employee. Do a little each day and inside a week it knows your business better than most people you could hire.
